AQUATIC SAFETY / IOT

AquaGuard

Software foundation built · pre-prototype

A facility-scale aquatic safety platform built around earlier signals, swimmer context and faster staff response.

~300,000estimated drowning deaths worldwide each year
~24%of drowning deaths are among children under five
57%of drowning deaths are among people aged 0–29

The problem is not simply whether a pool has a lifeguard or a camera. There can still be a critical gap between something starting to go wrong in the water and the facility understanding what is happening, where the swimmer is and how staff should respond.

AquaGuard is being designed as an integrated wearable-and-facility system: combine multiple swimmer signals, add location and movement context, identify potentially dangerous patterns earlier, and route the right information to staff through a defined escalation workflow.

The facility side is intended to provide more than an alarm. The goal includes swimmer/location context, device health, escalation records, event history and an operational evidence trail.

Market gap: based on the landscape reviewed so far, there are point solutions in drowning detection and pool safety, but we have not identified a like-for-like commercial system combining the full AquaGuard model — wearable multi-sensor intelligence, swimmer location, facility infrastructure, device-fleet monitoring, escalation workflows and auditable software — as one integrated operating layer.

The software foundation and operating logic already exist. The next major step is completing the integrated physical prototype, waterproof engineering and controlled pool validation, followed by a facility pilot.

Current focus

  • Integrated prototype engineering
  • Waterproof and controlled-pool validation
  • Manufacturing path and pilot preparation

What could help next

  • Funding
  • Hardware / manufacturing partners
  • Pilot facility and validation partners

Public-health figures: World Health Organization — Drowning fact sheet. AquaGuard is intended as a safety aid and does not replace lifeguards, supervision or emergency procedures.

EMERGING INTERFACES / DIGITAL SCENT

Cento

Research · validation · partner stage

An exploration of the “playback layer” for digital scent.

The idea was triggered by research from Osmo. Their “Scent Teleportation” work has demonstrated a laboratory workflow in which a real-world smell can be sampled, analysed through GC-MS and AI-based odor mapping, then reconstructed through automated formulation.

That raised a practical product question: if images have screens and sound has speakers, what could the consumer and commercial playback layer for scent look like?

Cento explores that layer — a connected scent device and software platform able to receive a digital instruction and reproduce an appropriate scent as part of an experience. Potential use cases include perfume discovery, hospitality, retail, museums, training and immersive entertainment.

The goal is not to reproduce Osmo's laboratory research. It is to investigate the product, device and commercial interface that could sit downstream from advances in scent digitization.

Current focus

  • Technical feasibility and use-case validation
  • Hardware concept
  • R&D partnership model

What could help next

  • Scent-technology / R&D partner
  • Hardware development partner
  • Prototype funding
